Almost every government exam in India asks for a photograph, and almost every form season the same problem appears: the portal says **"Invalid file size"** or **"Image dimensions not as per specification"**, and the application gets stuck a day before the deadline.

The good news is that photo requirements follow the same few rules everywhere. Once you understand them, you can prepare a photo for any exam in about two minutes.

## Step 1: Read three numbers from your notification

Open the official notification PDF and search (Ctrl + F) for the word "photograph". You are looking for three things:

1. **File size range in KB** — for example 20 KB to 50 KB. Both ends matter: a file that is *too small* is rejected just like one that is too big.
2. **Dimensions** — sometimes given in pixels (200 × 230 px), sometimes in centimetres (3.5 cm × 4.5 cm), and sometimes not given at all.
3. **Format** — nearly always JPG/JPEG.

Note these down. If the notice gives centimetres instead of pixels, read our guide on [KB, pixels, cm and DPI](/blog/photo-size-kb-pixels-dpi-explained) to convert them.

> Several 2026 notifications (SSC, Railway and UP Police) now capture your photo **live with the webcam** during the application. If your notice says this, you do not upload a photo at all — only the signature. See our [live photo capture tips](/blog/live-photo-capture-tips-ssc-railway).

## Step 2: Take a photo that will pass manual checks

File size is checked by software, but your photo is also checked by people at the document verification stage. A photo that is technically the right size but looks wrong can still cause trouble later.

- **Background:** plain white or very light, no patterns, no shadows behind your head.
- **Lighting:** face a window in daylight. Avoid a single bulb above you, which creates shadows under the eyes.
- **Pose:** look straight at the camera, both ears visible, mouth closed, neutral expression.
- **Framing:** your face should fill about 70–80% of the height of the photo.
- **No caps, dark glasses or filters.** Spectacles are usually allowed if there is no glare, but many notices now ask you to remove them.
- **Recency:** many exams require a photo taken within a few months, and UPSC asks for one taken within days of applying, with your name and date printed on it.

You do not need a studio. A phone camera held at eye level, about an arm's length away, is enough.

## Step 3: Crop and resize to the exact pixels

Open the [photo resizer](/tools/photo-resizer) and choose your exam preset. If your exam is not listed, choose **Custom size** and type the width and height from the notice.

Use the **Zoom** and **Move** sliders so your face is centred. The tool crops to the correct shape (aspect ratio) automatically, so your face will not look stretched.

If the notice gives no pixel size, a 4:5 shape such as 400 × 500 px is a safe choice for passport-style photos.

## Step 4: Hit the KB range

The tool tries many JPEG quality levels and keeps the sharpest one that is still under the maximum. If the result is below the minimum (this happens with small images), it adds harmless padding to the file so it meets the minimum. The picture itself does not change.

Look for the green **"within limit"** label before downloading.

## Step 5: Upload and double-check the preview

After uploading, most portals show a preview. Check that:

- your face is not cut off,
- the photo is not rotated sideways (phone photos sometimes are),
- it is the photo, not the signature, in the photo box — swapping them is a surprisingly common mistake.

Take a screenshot of the final application page with the photo visible. It is useful if there is any dispute later.

## Common reasons photos are rejected

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| "File size exceeds limit" | Lower the max KB slightly below the limit (e.g. 48 KB for a 50 KB cap) and download again. |
| "File size too small" | Set the minimum KB in the tool; it will pad the file. |
| "Invalid dimensions" | Use the exact pixel size from the notice, not an approximate one. |
| Blurry face | Start from a sharper original photo; compression cannot add detail. |
| Photo rejected at verification | Background not plain, face too small, or an old photo. Retake it. |

For a longer list, read [why exam form photos get rejected](/blog/why-exam-form-photos-get-rejected).

## Keep a copy

Save the final JPG and a few prints. You will often need the **same photo** on the admit card, at the exam centre and during document verification. Using a different photo later can create problems with identity checks.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Read the KB range, dimensions and format from the notification
- [ ] Photo on a plain light background, face straight, good light
- [ ] Resized with the correct preset or custom size
- [ ] Result shows "within limit"
- [ ] Preview checked after upload
- [ ] Final file and prints saved

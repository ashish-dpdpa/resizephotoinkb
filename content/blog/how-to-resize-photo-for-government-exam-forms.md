If you've ever filled an exam form close to the last date, you probably know the message: **"Invalid file size."** You pick a perfectly good photo, the portal rejects it, and nothing on the screen tells you what's actually wrong.

Almost every time, the fix is one of three things. The file is too heavy (or too light) in KB, the width and height in pixels don't match, or the photo itself breaks a rule like a dark background. Once you know which numbers the form is checking, getting it right takes a couple of minutes.

## Start with the notice, not the photo

Open the notification PDF and press Ctrl+F for "photograph". Most notices give you some mix of these:

- a **file size range**, like 20 KB to 50 KB
- a **size in pixels** (200 × 230 px) or in **centimetres** (3.5 × 4.5 cm)
- a **format**, which is nearly always JPG

Write them down somewhere. Pay attention to the lower limit too. A lot of people only look at the maximum and then wonder why a 12 KB photo bounces when the minimum was 20 KB.

If the notice gives centimetres instead of pixels, our explainer on [KB, pixels, cm and DPI](/blog/photo-size-kb-pixels-dpi-explained) shows how to convert them.

One thing has changed recently. SSC, the railway boards and UP Police now take your photo **live with the camera** during the form, so for those you don't upload a photo file at all, only the signature. If that's your exam, skip to our [live photo tips](/blog/live-photo-capture-tips-ssc-railway).

## Take a photo that will survive a human check

Software checks the file size. People check the photo, usually at document verification, months later. A photo that passes the portal can still cause trouble there, so it's worth getting it right now.

Stand in front of a plain white or light wall and face a window so daylight falls on your face. Hold the phone at eye level, about an arm's length away, and look straight at the lens. Both ears should be visible. Take off caps and dark glasses, and switch off beauty filters or portrait blur. Your face should fill most of the frame, roughly 70 to 80 percent of the height.

Recency matters as well. Several exams want a recent photo, and UPSC specifically asks for one taken within about ten days of applying, with your name and the date printed on it. Our [name and date tool](/tools/photo-with-name-and-date) handles that part.

## Resize it to the exact size

Open the [photo resizer](/tools/photo-resizer) and pick your exam from the list. The pixel size and KB range fill in automatically. If your exam isn't listed, choose Custom size and type the numbers from the notice.

Drag the photo inside the frame so your face sits in the middle, and use the zoom slider if there's too much background. The tool crops to the right shape, so your face won't get squashed or stretched.

As you adjust, the KB meter on the right updates. It tries different quality levels and keeps the sharpest one that's still under the maximum. If the result comes out below the minimum, which happens a lot with small images, it pads the file up to the limit without changing the picture. When the meter says **within limit**, download the JPG.

## Check it after uploading

Most portals show a small preview once the file is uploaded. Look at it properly before you pay the fee. Is your face cut off? Is the photo sideways? Is it actually the photo in the photo box, and not your signature? That last swap happens more often than you'd think, especially on a phone.

Take a screenshot of the final page with your photo visible. If there's ever a dispute about what you uploaded, you'll be glad you did.

## When it still gets rejected

| What the portal says | What usually fixes it |
|---|---|
| File size exceeds the limit | Set the max a little below the limit, e.g. 48 KB for a 50 KB cap |
| File size too small | Enter the minimum in the tool so it pads the file |
| Invalid dimensions | Use the exact pixels from the notice, not "roughly" the same |
| Photo looks blurry | Start from a sharper original; compression can't add detail |

There's a longer list in [why exam form photos get rejected](/blog/why-exam-form-photos-get-rejected).

## Keep the same photo for everything

Save the final JPG and get a few prints made. You'll usually need the same photo on the admit card, at the exam centre and at verification, and switching to a different photo halfway through is an easy way to get questioned.

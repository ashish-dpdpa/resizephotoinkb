Exam portals are fussy about formats. Upload a PNG where a JPG is expected and you'll get "invalid file type", even if the picture is perfect and well under the size limit. The rule of thumb is short, and the reasons behind it are worth a couple of minutes.

| What you're uploading | Use |
|---|---|
| Photograph | JPG / JPEG |
| Signature, thumb impression, handwritten declaration | JPG / JPEG, unless the notice says otherwise |
| Certificates, mark sheets, caste or income certificates | PDF (sometimes JPG) |
| Screenshots and graphics outside of forms | PNG |

## JPG is made for photos

JPG throws away detail your eye is unlikely to notice, which is why the files are small. That's exactly what a portal wants: a recognisable face in 20 to 50 KB.

Two things worth knowing. First, .jpg and .jpeg are the same format, so if a form says JPEG, your .jpg file is fine. Second, every time you save a JPG again it loses a little more, so always go back to the original photo instead of re-compressing an already compressed copy.

## PNG keeps everything, which is the problem

PNG stores every pixel exactly. That's lovely for screenshots, logos and text, but a photo saved as PNG is often five to ten times bigger than the same photo as JPG. Many portals won't take PNG at all.

If you've got a PNG, run it through the [photo resizer](/tools/photo-resizer) or one of the [compress tools](/tools/compress-image-to-50kb). They always save JPG.

And renaming `photo.png` to `photo.jpg` doesn't convert anything. The file is still a PNG inside, and some portals check.

## HEIC from iPhones

Newer iPhones save photos as HEIC to save space, and a lot of Indian portals don't understand it. The quick way around it is to open the photo in our tools in Safari. The browser converts it, and you download a JPG. To stop it happening in future, go to Settings, then Camera, then Formats, and choose Most Compatible. New photos will be saved as JPG.

## WebP

WebP is a newer web format. You'll usually end up with one after saving an image from a website or from WhatsApp Web. Treat it like PNG and convert it to JPG before uploading.

## PDF is for documents

PDF keeps multiple pages together and keeps text sharp, which is why portals use it for certificates, mark sheets and experience letters. Limits are usually 100 KB, 200 KB or 500 KB per file.

A few things help keep PDFs small:

- Use your phone's document scanner (Google Drive, Files by Google and the iPhone Notes app all have one). It crops and flattens the page for you.
- Pick black-and-white or greyscale for text documents. It cuts the size a lot.
- Only include the pages the form actually asks for. One stray blank page can push you over.
- If it's still too big, use [Compress PDF](/tools/compress-pdf) and pick your form's limit.

If a form wants a certificate as a JPG instead, photograph or scan it and use the [compress tool](/tools/compress-image-to-100kb) to hit the KB limit. And if you've got photos that need to become a PDF, [Image to PDF](/tools/image-to-pdf) does that.

## Before you upload

Check what format each upload needs in the notice. Photos and signatures should be JPG, inside the KB range and pixel size. Documents should be PDF (or JPG if asked), under the limit, with every page readable. Keep file names short and plain, like `photo.jpg`, `sign.jpg` or `10th-marksheet.pdf`.
